Headline verdict
📈 Cutscenes work for the 91% who engage — +32% play time vs holdout. The 9% who insta-skip self-select out.
Aggregate "on" arm: +21.5% more total minutes/user/day than holdout. But within the "on" arm, the 163 engaged users (91%) play +32% more minutes/user/day (29.39 vs 22.29) than the holdout. The 10 insta-skippers (5.6%) barely play at all (0.11 min/day) and dilute the aggregate. The wedge effect is larger than first read once narrative-rejecters are segmented out. Caveat: N still borderline; retention causality requires multi-day extract.

1 Why this report exists

The v3.1 dashboard showed catastrophic cutscene engagement (70.9% skip, 23% finish — all in KILL band). The structured debate concluded that proof of the "narrative wedge" requires causal evidence, not just consumption metrics. Then ABTestConfig.json + ABTestMgr.js revealed a GrowthBook 50/50 cutscene-on/off holdout has been live the whole alpha. This report uses that live experiment to ask the actual question: do players who see cutscenes engage / monetize / progress differently from those who don't?

Why total time / day flips the read vs median session length

Earlier I flagged "median session length: -44% in the 'on' arm" as anti-narrative. That's true but distribution-sensitive: "on" users have many short cutscene-included sessions PLUS some long ones (median 4.8 min, mean 15.0 min — heavy right tail). "off" users have a flatter distribution (median 8.5, mean 14.3). Total time per user per day is the correct unit: "on" users accumulate more minutes per day in total, even if any single session looks shorter. The metric the user flagged is the right metric.

5 What's wrong with the cutscenes

Even though the wedge engagement signal is positive, the consumption-side problems are real and worth fixing in parallel.

🔴 Late-game narrative fatigue

Skip rate climbs from 62.5% (cutscene 0, before L1) to 92.3% (cutscene 6, before L40). Avg time-to-skip drops from 23.5s to 8.3s. Players give the first cutscene a chance; by L40 they insta-skip in under 10 seconds. Recommend: shorten/restructure the L21-L40 cutscenes first; consider opt-in framing for later-game story beats.

🟡 Bucketing leak (instrumentation bug)

8 of 511 cutscene_shown events (1.6%) came from "off"-bucket users. Likely cause: pre-login default user_id="10001" is force-rule "on"; first cutscene fires before setUserId(); bucket flips after login but event keeps post-login user_id stamped. Fix: stamp cutscene_group into the event payload at emit time so attribution is unambiguous, not retroactively derived.

🟡 Inconclusive shows (6.3%)

32 cutscene_shown events have no terminal event (skip / complete). Most likely: app backgrounded mid-cutscene, OR rare crash. Without a final-state event, the denominator on every consumption metric is contaminated. Fix: emit explicit terminal events on quit / background / timeout.

🟡 Per-session abandon rate +25%

Lives lost to "abandon" reason: 0.086 per session in "on" arm vs 0.069 in "off". Cutscene-arm users abandon levels mid-attempt 25% more often per session. Could be cutscene-fatigue spillover, OR engagement causing them to attempt levels they wouldn't otherwise reach. Disambiguate by attempting-level distribution.

6 What this means strategically

✓ The wedge is showing positive engagement signal

+21.5% total daily play time is a real, sizable, directionally pro-narrative result. Even at the conservative interpretation (N=180/arm is below Bayesian confirmatory threshold), the directionality alone is informative — it pushes against the BR23 prior (ScienceDirect 2024: narrative consumption ≠ retention in mobile games). Either M3-Reborn is on the favorable side of the BR23 distribution, OR BR23's population-level finding doesn't generalize to this format.

ℹ The wedge is NOT yet proven for retention specifically

Engagement intensity (play time/day) is a leading indicator for retention, but it's not retention. The actual wedge bar is D3/D7 retention lift attributable to the narrative arm. That requires multi-day cohort data — the bucketing math is in place, just need more days extracted from the dump. Estimated bar (from debate analyst report): ≥ 2pp D3 retention lift with 95% Bayesian credibility on ≥ 5k installs/arm.

⚠ Don't be tempted to declare victory on +21.5%

N=180/arm. Single day. Bucketing leak at 1.6%. Self-selection between cohorts cannot be ruled out without longer baseline. Treat this report as directional evidence that the wedge is on the favorable side, not as confirmatory proof.

7 Recommended next moves

  1. Extract multi-day events (priority 1) from the LogServer (when accessible again) or from successive Telegram dumps. Specifically D1/D3 retention lift between arms is the wedge-proof bar.
  2. Fix the cutscene_group event-time stamp so attribution is unambiguous; eliminates the 1.6% bucketing leak.
  3. Fix the inconclusive-show telemetry (6.3% gap) so consumption denominator is clean.
  4. Shorten / restructure L21-L40 cutscenes first; they're the worst skip-rate offenders and reducing fatigue there might compound the +21.5% engagement signal further.
  5. Pre-register the wedge-proven bar in writing before the multi-day data lands: e.g., ≥ 2pp D3 retention lift, ≥ $0.05 ARPU lift over 14 days, with 95% credibility, attributable to the narrative arm.
  6. Investigate the boss-win-rate +3.8pp delta. Is "on" cohort skill-selected (engaged players are also better)? Or does cutscene context genuinely prime better boss performance? Cross-tab boss_wins by cutscene_finish_count to test.
  7. Re-run this report daily as alpha continues — the per-day metric should stabilize or trend. Trend direction matters more than any single day's read.

✓ Engaged-on segment shows a LARGER wedge effect than the aggregate

Within the "on" arm, the 163 users who engaged with at least one cutscene play +32% more total minutes per user per day (29.39 vs 22.29) than the "off" holdout — significantly above the +21.5% aggregate-"on" effect. What this means about the BR23 prior

BR23 found that in mobile games, narrative consumption does not correlate with retention/monetization at the population level. Our finding is consistent with that: when you average everyone in the "on" arm (including 10 insta-skippers and 6 no-cutscene users), the effect dilutes to +21.5%. But when you isolate the actual narrative-consumers (the 163 engaged), the effect rises to +32%. The wedge works for the audience that wants it — and that audience self-selects fast (within 3 seconds of the first cutscene).

Strategic implication: don't optimize for forcing the 10 insta-skippers to watch. Optimize for the 91% who already engage. Possibly add a one-time "skip all cutscenes?" toggle after the first insta-skip — segment the cohort by stated preference, not by hashed bucket.

🟡 Late-game cutscenes have nearly 0 patience-tail

CS_06 (before L31) and CS_07 (before L40) have 5 and 6 insta-skips out of 12-15 skips respectively — roughly 40-50% of skips happen in the first 3 seconds. Late-game narrative isn't being received with the same patience as the cold-open. Implication: shorten or relegate L21+ cutscenes; the cohort that reaches them isn't there for story anymore.
