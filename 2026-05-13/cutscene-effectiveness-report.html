5 What's wrong with the cutscenes

Even though the wedge engagement signal is positive, the consumption-side problems are real and worth fixing in parallel.

🔴 Late-game narrative fatigue

Skip rate climbs from … (first cutscene, before L…) to … (last cutscene, before L…). Avg time-to-skip drops from …s to …s. Players give the first cutscene a chance; by the late game they pattern-match "more story" and reject quickly. Recommend: shorten/restructure the late-game cutscenes first; consider opt-in framing for later story beats.

🟡 Bucketing leak (instrumentation bug)

… of … cutscene_shown events (…) came from "off"-bucket users. Likely cause: pre-login default user_id="10001" is force-rule "on"; first cutscene fires before setUserId(); bucket flips after login but event keeps post-login user_id stamped. Fix: stamp cutscene_group into the event payload at emit time so attribution is unambiguous, not retroactively derived.

6 What this means strategically

✓ The wedge is showing positive engagement signal

… total daily play time is the aggregate-arm read. Even at the conservative interpretation (N≈…/arm is below Bayesian confirmatory threshold), the directionality is informative — it weighs against the BR23 prior (ScienceDirect 2024: narrative consumption ≠ retention in mobile games). Either M3-Reborn is on the favorable side of the BR23 distribution, OR BR23's population-level finding doesn't generalize to this format.

ℹ The wedge is NOT yet proven for retention specifically

Engagement intensity (play time/day) is a leading indicator for retention, but it's not retention. The actual wedge bar is D3/D7 retention lift attributable to the narrative arm. That requires multi-day cohort data — the bucketing math is in place, just need more days extracted from the dump. Estimated bar (from debate analyst report): ≥ 2pp D3 retention lift with 95% Bayesian credibility on ≥ 5k installs/arm.

⚠ Don't be tempted to declare victory on the aggregate lift

N≈…/arm. Single day. Bucketing leak at …. Self-selection between cohorts cannot be ruled out without longer baseline. Treat this report as directional evidence, not as confirmatory proof.

9 Refined skip rate — what does "skip" actually mean?

The headline 70% aggregate skip rate lumps together two very different player behaviors. Separating "rejected on sight" (≤3s) from "watched then bailed" (>3s) reveals the design problem is mostly about finish, not start.

Two layered definitions of "skip"

Game-side (binary, no threshold). The game emits cutscene_skipped whenever the player hits the skip button, regardless of timing. The secondsIntoPlayback field records WHEN within the scene runtime. A skip at 0.5s and a skip at 30s of a 35s scene are both "skips" in the raw data.

Analysis-side (3s threshold, configurable). We split cutscene_skipped events into:

  • Insta-reject (secondsIntoPlayback ≤ 3) — rejected on sight, never engaged with the content
  • Engaged-skip (secondsIntoPlayback > 3) — watched at least a few seconds, then bailed before the end

The 3-second threshold is a heuristic (ENGAGEMENT_THRESHOLD_SEC in analyze-cutscene-segments.js). Tune to 2s or 5s and re-run to test sensitivity.

The reframe — most "skippers" are NOT rejecting the narrative

Of all cutscene shows, only a small minority are insta-rejected (skipped in ≤3 seconds). The bulk of "skips" come from players who watched part of the scene before bailing. This shifts the design problem: from "players don't want narrative" (which would imply cutting it) to "players engage but don't finish" (which implies shortening scenes, tightening hooks, or earning watch-through with better pacing).

🟡 Late-game scenes have a different problem

Insta-reject share climbs sharply on late-game cutscenes (L21+). The cold-open earns attention; later scenes get pattern-matched as "more story, skip it." Late-game narrative needs either much shorter runtime or a different framing trigger.

10 Skip rate by main event — did users see the story beat?

Each cutscene has a designated main-event timestamp — the narrative beat where the meaningful story actually lands. A skip before that timestamp means the user missed the point; a skip after means they at least saw the beat before bailing. This converts the raw skip rate into a "missed the story" rate, which is closer to what narrative designers actually care about.

Methodology

Per-cutscene main-event timestamps are provided as authored config (not derived). Skip-time bucket counts are converted to pre / post-threshold counts via linear interpolation within bucket boundaries (skips in [10–20s] split linearly around an in-bucket threshold). The 30s+ bucket is treated as fully post-threshold since all 7 thresholds fall at ≤24s. Inconclusive shows (no terminal event) are reported separately — they didn't yield a measurable outcome.

Reading guide

Compare the Missed beat % column to the raw skip rate in Section 4. The gap = users who skipped but DID see the main beat — those are not narrative-rejecters, just impatient finishers. A cutscene where missed-beat% ≈ skip% means most skippers cut out before the beat. A cutscene where missed-beat% << skip% means designers got the beat in early enough that most "skippers" actually consumed the story.
